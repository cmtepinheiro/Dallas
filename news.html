Autonomous helicopters: press coverage and technical notes

Third-party coverage of Dallas Autonomous — also written Dallas Autonomus — and short explainers on how autonomous helicopters (VTOL UAVs) work and where they fit.

IN THE PRESS
Agência FAPESP · 8 May 2024 · By Roseli Andrion

Brazilian startup designs self-flying aerial vehicle for crop spraying

Agência FAPESP reports on how a coffee grower in Minas Gerais commissioned an autonomous vertical take-off and landing (VTOL) aircraft from the Dallas Autonomus team. On steep hillsides tractors struggle and conventional crop-dusting aircraft are unsuitable, while drones are limited in load capacity and battery life. The article explains why a helicopter was chosen — among other reasons, its slower flight and rotor downwash cause less spray drift — and mentions the partnership with ITA (Aeronautical Technology Institute) and the use of professional aviation components. It adds that the same aircraft can serve sugarcane, soybean and orange crops.

TECHNICAL NOTES

Autonomous helicopters, explained

What is an autonomous helicopter (VTOL UAV)?

It is a helicopter that flies its mission without a pilot on board. An autopilot follows the planned route using satellite and inertial navigation (GNSS + INS) while an operator supervises from a ground control station. Because it takes off and lands vertically (VTOL), it needs no runway. Dallas Autonomous is developing a platform in the 500 kg class (maximum take-off weight).

Why use a helicopter instead of a multirotor drone?

Multirotor drones are practical for lighter work but, as co-founder Marcus Prianti told Agência FAPESP, they have limited load capacity and battery life. A fuel-powered helicopter swaps batteries for fuel: the Dallas platform is designed for a 130–300 kg payload, up to 7 hours of endurance and a 500 km autonomous mission range.

Why is a helicopter well suited to spraying on steep terrain?

According to Agência FAPESP, tractors have difficulty on steep hillsides and crop-dusting aircraft are unsuitable on complex topography. A helicopter can fly slowly, hover and reach hard-to-access areas, and its slower flight and rotor downwash reduce spray drift.

Which missions can one airframe cover?

The platform is designed as one airframe with interchangeable payloads — imaging payloads, cargo compartment and hooks, medical stretcher, auxiliary fuel tanks and a spray bar, among others. Target missions are defense (ISR, logistics, force protection), offshore (cargo and equipment transfer to platforms) and agribusiness (heavy-duty aerial support).

What is the development status?

The company was founded in February 2021, the project was approved for Phase II of FAPESP's PIPE program in January 2022 and the prototype completed its first flight test in October 2022. Today the platform is at TRL-6, under ongoing flight validation.

Is it “Dallas Autonomous” or “Dallas Autonomus”?

Both spellings refer to the same company. FAPESP records and press coverage write Dallas Autonomus (company name: Dallas Autonomus Ltda); this site also uses Dallas Autonomous.
